# Design System: The Trailprint Atlas

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Survey Volume"**

The Atlas is a bound cartographic survey, opened on a desk. Not a website that borrows
map imagery — a *volume*, whose parts are named the way a surveyor's would be: leaves and
spreads, plates with engraved collars, courses, stations, traverses, benchmarks, mounts,
loose sheets. That vocabulary is not decoration applied afterwards; it is the structure
the code is actually built on, which is why the system holds together across pages that
look nothing alike.

The material is paper and brass. Every surface is a parchment tone; every division is a
thin ruled line rather than a box; every label is small, uppercase, widely letterspaced
and the colour of an engraving rather than of text. Depth comes from sheets resting on
sheets, never from a floating card with a drop shadow. Where the subject is a place, the
land *is* the page and the instruments float on it; where the subject is a person or a
record, the page is a leaf of the book.

The palette is deliberately low-contrast and warm. It is the identity, not an oversight,
and it does not get darkened to satisfy a ratio. Colour carries exactly one piece of
information: **ink is keyed to the year a hike was walked**, generated by formula so a new
year needs no edit.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Parchment and brass; nothing is pure white and nothing is pure black
- Ruled lines, not boxes; engraved labels, not headings
- Depth by paper layering, not by elevation
- Colour means *when*, not *what kind*
- Motion is organic — things settle, they never snap or bounce
- Every crossing between pages is the same brass door

## Colors

Warm, low-contrast, and grounded in the natural world the Atlas records: forest inks,
sun-bleached parchments, weathered brass. **These are the one normative palette.** The
older earthy tokens still declared in `base.css` (`--trail-green` `#4a7c59`, `--evergreen`
`#2c3e50`, `--paper` `#f9f9f9`, `--mist` `#eee`) are **legacy**: they survive only on pages
not yet rebuilt (credits, echoes, parts of map) and retire when the last one converts.

### Primary
- **Deep Forest Ink** (`#24401f`): the darkest thing on the page. Headings, engraved
  wordmarks, the door's hover fill, primary type on parchment. Used where black would be
  used, and never black.
- **Trail Green Ink** (`#2f5c40`): the working ink. Links, drawn trails, lit marks, the
  green that says "this one". Lighter and more alive than Deep Forest.

### Secondary
- **Brass** (`#a5854a`): fittings, rails, knobs, hardware. The only metal in the system.
  **Brass Highlight** (`#cfae6e`) and **Brass Shadow** (`#7a5f36`) turn it three-dimensional;
  **Deep Brass** (`#7d6231`) sets engraved numerals and hairline keylines.

### Tertiary
- **Margin Red** (`#b4645c`): the annotation a hand made in the margin. Extremely rare, and
  its rarity is the point — a tally mark, a correction, a note. Never a warning colour.

### Neutral
- **Engraved Grey-Tan** (`#9a927c`): the most-used colour in the Atlas. Every small
  uppercase label, collar, caption and axis tick. It reads as printed *into* the paper.
- **Stone** (`#6f6a58`): secondary prose, italic sub-lines, the written voice at rest.
- **Rule Tan** (`#d8ccae`): every ruled line, divider, paper edge and dashed border.
- **Card Paper** (`#fffdf6`): the brightest surface — a plate, a card, a mount's window.
  Stands in for white and is never `#fff`.
- **Parchment** (`#f4ecd8`), **Leaf Parchment** (`#efe5cc`), **Warm Cream** (`#fbf7ea`),
  **Cream** (`#fbf6e6`), **Mid Parchment** (`#ece3ce`), **Deep Parchment** (`#e6dcc0`): the
  page itself, from lightest to most weathered. Leaves are built as multi-stop gradients
  across these, not as flat fills.

### The Desk
Not paper, and not a background — the **surface the volume rests on**. It is always
darker and less saturated than any parchment, so the page reads as an object lying on
something rather than as a colour field.
- **Lit Desk** (`#b8a67e`), with **Desk Highlight** (`#c8b892`) and **Desk Shade**
  (`#97865f`): a tan surface under a light, laid as a radial gradient so the volume sits
  in a pool of it. The crew volume's desk.
- **Wood Desk** (`#3b2d1d`): a dark, warm hardwood, used where the page is a working
  surface seen from directly above rather than a book — the hike page's Light Table.

### Named Rules

**The Desk Rule.** A volume always rests on something, and that something is never paper.
If a page introduces a new backdrop it belongs to this family: darker than parchment, low
saturation, and lit from one direction so the object on it casts a warm shadow.

**The No-White, No-Black Rule.** `#fff` and `#000` never appear as surface or type colours.
The brightest surface is Card Paper (`#fffdf6`); the darkest ink is Deep Forest (`#24401f`).
Pure values read as screen, and this is paper. (Pure black is permitted only inside a
mask, gradient stop, or an SVG filter, where it is maths and not a colour.)

**The Year-Ink Rule.** Hue carries one meaning and one only: the year a hike was walked,
generated by `atlasYearInk()` in `config.js`. Never hand-add a year colour, and never
introduce a second colour-coded dimension — a reader who has learned that colour means
*when* must not have to unlearn it.

**The Warm Shadow Rule.** Shadows are warm brown (`rgba(60, 45, 20, …)`), never neutral
grey or black. A neutral shadow on parchment reads as dirt.

## Typography

**Display Font:** National Park (variable 200–800, self-hosted) — modelled on the routed
lettering of US park signage.
**Body Font:** Alegreya Sans (400/700 + italics, self-hosted).

**Character:** A trail sign and a memoir. National Park is the carved public voice: what a
sign at a trailhead says. Alegreya Sans is the written private voice: what someone wrote
in a notebook about the day. The Atlas alternates between them constantly, and the switch
always means something.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (National Park, 700, `clamp(1.1rem, 2.4vw, 3rem)`): page titles, a person's
  name on their record, big stat numerals in the Odometer.
- **Title** (National Park, 700, ~18px, `0.04em`): panel and section headings.
- **Label** (Alegreya Sans, 700, 9.5–11px, uppercase, `0.16–0.22em`): collars, axis ticks,
  captions, the door's destination line. The most-repeated type in the system.
- **Body** (Alegreya Sans, 400, 12–13px): prose, list rows, field text.
- **Note** (Alegreya Sans, 400 *italic*, 11–11.5px, Stone): sub-lines, spans, provenance,
  the quiet second line under almost every heading.

### Named Rules

**The 18px Floor.** National Park is legible at 18px and up, and again at 8–10px **when it
is wide-tracked ALL CAPS** — the tracking and the caps carry it where the letterforms
alone would not. Between those, and in sentence case at any small size, it goes soft.

So: **18px+ for headings and numerals, or 8–10px as tracked caps. Never in between, never
mixed-case small.** Body text and prose are always Alegreya Sans.

*Written first as a flat "never below 18px", which measurement disproved: the codebase
sets the display face under 18px in 116 places, and the 66 that are tracked caps read
perfectly. The real offenders are the mixed-case ones — `observatory.css:193` sets a peak
label at 13.5px in sentence case and it is the softest type on the home page.*

**The Two Voices Rule.** Routed display type states; italic body type remarks. A heading
and its italic sub-line are the system's fundamental pairing, and almost every panel in
the Atlas is built from it.

## Layout

Pages are built as **fixed-viewport shells** — the window is the volume's cover and the
page does not scroll as a whole; content that overflows lives in an explicit internal
scroller. Eight of nine stylesheets do this, and it is the reason the Atlas reads as an
object rather than a document.

Two spatial models, chosen by subject:
- **The spread** (home, crew, crew-member): two leaves and a gutter, with a binding
  gradient down the middle. Columns are asymmetric (e.g. `0.88fr / 1.12fr`), and the
  binding must be positioned where the columns actually divide, not at 50%.
- **The land** (map, trip, hike): the map fills the frame edge to edge and instruments —
  decks, sheets, rails, plaques — float on top of it.

Rhythm is tight. Spacing runs 2 / 6 / 10 / 18 / 26px; panels sit on ruled lines rather
than in gaps. Wide charts are **re-drawn to the measured width, never scaled to it**.

**There is no global `box-sizing: border-box`.** One scoped exception exists at
`crew.css:52` for `.crew-book` and its descendants. Check which side of that line an
element is on before reasoning about widths.

**Responsive behaviour is desktop-only by product decision.** Layouts are fluid across
desktop widths (`clamp()`, `min()`, fr units) and re-measure on resize; no mobile
breakpoints exist and none should be added.

## Elevation & Depth

**The system is not elevated — it is layered.** Depth is sheets of paper resting on other
sheets: a plate on a leaf, a mount on a plate, a slide on a desk. The cue is a paper edge,
a ruled line, and a *very* low warm shadow — not a lifted card floating in space.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Seated** (`0 1px 3px rgba(60, 45, 20, 0.35)`): a mark or small object resting on paper.
- **Placed** (`0 2px 6px rgba(60, 45, 20, 0.28)`): a card or plate lying on a leaf.
- **Lifted** (`0 4px 14px rgba(60, 45, 20, 0.24)`): the one state where something is
  genuinely above the page — a door under the pointer, a lightbox.
- **Engraved** (`inset 0 0 0 2px rgba(255, 253, 246, 0.45)`): pressed *into* the surface
  rather than raised off it; used for windows and mounts.

### Named Rules

**The Flat-At-Rest Rule.** Surfaces are flat at rest. Depth appears as a response to state
— hover, focus, selection — or to say "this sheet is on top of that one". Nothing floats
merely to look important.

## Shapes

Nearly square. The form language is cut paper and engraved metal, so corners are small
(2px engraved, 8px for a door or card) and edges are ruled rather than outlined. Two
exceptions carry meaning: **circles** (`50%`) are always *marks* — a hike, a station, a
benchmark, a trailhead dot — and **pills** (`999px`) are always *chips*, a filter or a
name you can press.

A border is a hairline in Rule Tan. A dashed border means "this is provisional or
annotated" — a sighting, a journey line, a datum band.

## Components

### The Atlas Door *(signature)*
The single most important component: one brass-on-cream card behind **every** crossing
between pages, so moving between scales always reads as the same gesture.
- **Shape:** rounded card (8px), Card Paper fill, hairline Rule Tan edge
- **Content:** a chevron pointing the way, a label naming the **destination**, an italic
  sub-line, and a glyph depicting what you are opening
- **Hover:** fills Deep Forest Ink, type goes Cream, shadow deepens to Lifted, and the
  chevron travels 3px in its direction of travel
- **`.is-return`:** mirrors chevron and glyph for the journey back
- **Rule:** only *sizes* are set per page. Never restyle one door alone.

### Plate + Collar *(signature)*
Every panel in the Atlas is a plate wearing a collar: a small engraved label at the top
left, an optional count or note at the top right, a hairline rule beneath, then content.
- **Collar:** Label type in Engraved Grey-Tan, `0.16–0.2em` tracking
- **Rule:** 1px Rule Tan, full width
- **Body:** Card Paper on a parchment leaf

### The Mount
A photograph is never a scrim with type over it. It sits in a paper mount: a window with
an engraved inset edge above, and the caption printed on the paper below.

### Marks
A hike is a filled circle in its year's ink (11px), a chapter is a larger ringed circle
carrying its count (16px). Lit marks scale 1.5× and take a green halo; unlit marks in a
reading context drop to 0.28 opacity.

### Navigation
A single dark bar (`#333`) across the top, Display type, injected on every page by
`nav.js`. It is deliberately the one un-parchment surface in the Atlas — the cover's
spine, not part of the page.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** use the palette tokens above; the parchment world is normative and the `base.css`
  earthy tokens are legacy pending conversion.
- **Do** pair a Display/Title heading with an italic Note sub-line — it is the system's
  fundamental unit.
- **Do** set every small label in Alegreya Sans, uppercase, `0.16–0.22em`, Engraved Grey-Tan.
- **Do** convey depth with a paper edge and a warm low shadow.
- **Do** re-draw a chart to its measured width on resize.
- **Do** route every page-to-page crossing through `.atlas-door`.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use `#fff` or `#000` as a surface or type colour.
- **Don't** set National Park below 18px.
- **Don't** add a second colour-coded dimension; hue means the year, and only the year.
- **Don't** float a panel or follow the cursor with one — the Atlas answers in place.
- **Don't** use emoji as an interface icon, ever. Hand-drawn SVG or an empty slot.
- **Don't** add a bare `100vh` shell without a `min-height` floor.
- **Don't** introduce a new easing curve or duration; use the motion scale below.

## Motion

*Established 8 August 2026. Before this there was no motion system: nine different
easing curves and ten-plus durations across the codebase, with the most common transition
being the browser default `ease` — which accelerates and decelerates with equal weight and
is exactly what reads as machine-made.*

**Motion in the Atlas is organic.** Things come to rest the way something does in the
physical world: quick to leave, long to settle, never a bounce, never a spring, never
linear. Nature has no constant velocity and no overshoot, and neither does this.

### Easing
- **Settle** (`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`): the default, and the curve for anything
  *arriving* — a panel opening, a page scrolling to an answer, a card landing. Already the
  codebase's dominant considered curve.
- **Leave** (`cubic-bezier(0.55, 0, 0.85, 0.35)`): for anything *departing*. Departures
  accelerate away; only arrivals get the long tail.
- **Veil** (`cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.25, 1)`): the cut behind a veil — a hard in, a hold, a
  soft out. Reserved for camera changes on the map-shaped pages.

### Duration
- **Quick** (`180ms`): a state change with no travel — colour, opacity, a ruled line.
- **Settle** (`380ms`): something moving a short distance into place.
- **Travel** (`700ms`): something crossing the page — a veil, a page turn, a leaf scrolling.

### Named Rules

**The No-Default-Ease Rule.** Never write bare `ease`, `ease-in-out`, or `linear`. Every
transition names one of the three curves above. Bare `ease` is the single largest cause of
the Atlas feeling mechanical.

**The Reduced-Motion Rule.** Every animated thing has a `prefers-reduced-motion` answer,
and the answer lives with the component's own definition — not in a page stylesheet that
covers one page while the shared component keeps moving everywhere else.
